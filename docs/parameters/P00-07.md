## In plain words

The encoder is the eye of the servo. It measures the position of the motor shaft many thousands of times each second. There are two main types: incremental and absolute.

## How it works

- **Incremental encoder** (0 or 1): it sends a pulse for each small step of movement. The drive counts the pulses. After power-off, the count is lost, so the machine must home again.
- **Absolute encoder, single-turn** (2): it sends the exact angle in one turn as a number. It knows the angle at power-on, but not the number of turns.
- **Absolute encoder, multi-turn** (3): it also counts the turns. With a battery, it keeps the count while the power is off.

A typical absolute encoder has 131 072 positions in one turn. That is 17 bits.

## On your machine

This value comes from the motor. Do not change it. A wrong value gives an encoder alarm, and the motor does not run.
