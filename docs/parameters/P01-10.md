## In plain words

A limit switch near each end of an axis tells the drive "stop, you are at the end". This parameter selects what the motor does after this stop: hold its position, or go limp.

## How it works

When a limit input operates, the drive stops the motor. After that, it accepts only commands that move the axis away from the limit.

- **0 Coast**: the motor has no current after the stop. The axis is free and can drift.
- **1 Hold position**: the motor stays energized and holds the axis where it stopped. This is the factory setting.

The limit inputs are DI3 and DI4 by default (P06-05 and P06-07).

## On your machine

Many LinuxCNC machines connect the limit switches to LinuxCNC, not to the drive. Then this setting has no effect.

If the limit switches connect to the drive, keep 1. A vertical axis must not coast, because it falls when the motor has no current.
