## In plain words

An incremental encoder has a disc with many fine lines. A light shines through the disc, and a sensor counts the lines that pass. More lines give a finer measurement.

## How it works

The encoder gives two signals, A and B, that are a quarter of a line apart. The drive counts each rising and falling edge of both signals. So it counts 4 edges for each line.

For example, an encoder with 2500 lines gives 2500 × 4 = 10 000 counts for each motor turn. The drive uses this number for all position calculations.

## On your machine

This value applies only to incremental encoders. Do not change it. A wrong value makes every distance wrong and can make the motor jerk at start-up.
