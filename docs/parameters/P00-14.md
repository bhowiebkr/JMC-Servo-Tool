## In plain words

At start-up, a motor with an incremental encoder does not know the exact angle of its rotor magnets. It only knows which of six sectors the rotor is in. This is start angle 3 of the six. The drive uses it when the rotor is in sector 3.

## How it works

Three signals from the motor, called U, V and W, divide one electrical turn into six sectors of 60°. Each sector has its own start angle. The drive puts the current in the coils at this angle until the encoder gives the first Z pulse. From then on, the drive uses the exact angle P00-11.

So the start angles only matter for a short time after power-on. During this time, the motor gives slightly less torque.

## On your machine

The motor maker measures these six angles and stores them. They apply only to incremental encoders. With an absolute encoder, the drive knows the exact angle at once, and these values have no effect.

Do not change it. A wrong angle can make the motor jerk or turn the wrong way at start-up.
