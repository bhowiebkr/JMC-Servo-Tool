## In plain words

A standard incremental encoder needs many signal wires. A wire-saving encoder sends the same information on fewer wires. This makes the cable thinner and cheaper.

## How it works

At start-up, a motor needs to know roughly where the magnets of the rotor are. A standard encoder sends this on three extra signals, called U, V and W. A wire-saving encoder sends this on the normal A and B wires for a short time after power-on. Then it changes to the normal A and B pulses.

The drive must know which type it has. Otherwise it reads the start signals as movement.

## On your machine

This setting applies only to incremental encoders (P00-07 = 0 or 1). It has no effect with an absolute encoder. Do not change it unless you change the motor.
