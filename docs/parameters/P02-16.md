## In plain words

The integral part of the speed loop collects every small speed error over time. This lets it remove an error that stays, for example from friction. But if the motor is held back for a long time, the integral keeps on growing. When the motor is free again, this stored value makes it overshoot. The integral limit stops this.

## How it works

Think of a bucket that collects water. The integral is the water level, and the error is the water that flows in. The limit is the height of the bucket. Without a limit, the bucket can fill without end. Then it takes a long time to empty, and the axis overshoots during this time.

This effect is called integrator wind-up. It happens when the motor is at its torque limit, for example in a hard acceleration or against a hard stop.

The drive data does not give the unit of this value.

## When to change it

Keep the factory value of 25000. A lower value reduces the overshoot after a jam or a hard acceleration. But a value that is too low stops the integral from holding a load against gravity. Change it only with the servo off.
