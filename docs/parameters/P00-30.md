## In plain words

When a motor slows down, it becomes a generator. The energy of the moving mass flows back into the drive and charges its capacitors. The voltage inside the drive then rises. A braking resistor turns this energy into heat, so the voltage stays safe.

## How it works

- **0**: the drive uses its internal resistor.
- **1**: the drive uses an external resistor. Then also set its power (P00-31) and its resistance (P00-32).
- **2**: no resistor. Use this only if the axis never returns much energy.

The drive calculates the heat in the resistor from the power and the resistance. If the resistor gets too much energy, the drive stops with the alarm AL.431. If the voltage rises too high, it stops with AL.402.

## On your machine

A light CNC axis returns little energy, so the internal resistor is usually enough. If you see the overvoltage alarm AL.402 during fast stops, add an external resistor. Slower deceleration in LinuxCNC also helps.

## Watch out

An external resistor gets very hot. Mount it on metal, away from wires and chips. Never set 2 if the drive has a heavy or vertical axis.
